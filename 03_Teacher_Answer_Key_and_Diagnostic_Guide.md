**Focus:** International Sales, Business Development & Overseas Operations

## 🔑 Part 1: Multiple Choice Solutions & Diagnostic Flags

| # | Correct Answer | Key Grammar Point | Diagnostic Flag (What an error means in Sales) |
| :-: | :-: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **B** (*is negotiating / handle*) | Present Continuous vs Present Simple | If missed: Student uses Simple for ongoing temporary deals or struggles to describe dynamic business situations. |
| **2** | **C** (*were preparing / asked*) | Past Continuous vs Past Simple | If missed: Difficulty narrating complex business background and sudden deal disruptions. |

## 🔍 Part 2: Error-Spotting Corrections & Underlying Issues

1. *"I am working in the overseas division since January 2021."*  
   * **Correction:** *"I **have worked** (or **have been working**) in the overseas division since January 2021."*  
   * **Sales Impact:** Essential for establishing credibility when introducing professional background to foreign clients.

2. *"We need to discuss about the pricing policy before the meeting with our foreign client."*  
   * **Correction:** *"We need to **discuss the pricing policy**..."* (remove *about*).  
   * **Sales Impact:** Eliminates classic non-native preposition clutter in boardroom discussions.

3. *"If the distributor will sign the contract by Friday, we will dispatch the first batch."*  
   * **Correction:** *"If the distributor **signs** the contract by Friday, we will dispatch..."*  
   * **Sales Impact:** Vital precision for written sales agreements, emails, and supply schedules.

4. *"I look forward to meet you at the international trade exhibition next month."*  
   * **Correction:** *"I look forward to **meeting** you..."*  
   * **Sales Impact:** The single most common email sign-off mistake in international sales communication.

5. *"The sales director explained me the new commission structure."*  
   * **Correction:** *"The sales director explained **to me** the new commission structure."*  
   * **Sales Impact:** Proper verb structure when reporting instructions or feedback from management.

## 📊 Score Interpretation & Recommended Trajectory

| Total Score (out of 20) | CEFR Level | Sales & Business English Focus |
| :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **0 – 8** | **A2+ / Early B1** | **Foundational Sales Communicator:** Focus on standard tenses (talking about products, past shipments, schedules). Drill *since/for* and question forms for client calls. |
| **9 – 14** | **Solid B1** | **Active Account Manager:** Can handle routine client emails and basic meetings, but makes predictable errors in conditionals, prepositions, and polite indirect phrasing. Target: Contract conditionals & diplomatic inquiry. |
| **15 – 18** | **B1+ / Entry B2** | **International Negotiator:** Strong core grammar. Needs refinement in high-stakes negotiations: nuanced modals (*might vs could*), hypothetical concession language (*"Provided that..."*, *"If we were to..."*), and formal passive contract terms. |
| **19 – 20** | **Solid B2 / C1** | **Global Commercial Strategist:** Mastered baseline grammar. Focus on persuasive discourse, idioms, softeners, objection handling under pressure, and cross-cultural executive presentations. |
