# Executive Master Dossier: English for Overseas Division & International Sales
**Course:** 1-on-1 Diagnostic & First Class (Online)  
**Target Profile:** International Sales & Business Development Manager (Overseas Markets)  
**CEFR Benchmark:** Diagnostic B1 to B2  
**Lesson Structure:** 30 Minutes Commercial Spoken Assessment & 30 Minutes Grammar Diagnostic (with 50% extra extension material)

---

# Section 1: Master Lesson Plan & Timetable (60 Minutes)

### Phase 1: Commercial Spoken Assessment & Needs Analysis (00:00 – 00:30)
* **00:00 – 00:05 (5 min): Rapport & Setup**
  * Welcome, verify audio/video, establish an informal, collaborative business atmosphere.
* **00:05 – 00:20 (15 min): Spoken Diagnostic Part 1 — Overseas Markets & Daily Sales Work**
  * *Prompt 1:* Could you introduce your role in the overseas division? Which international markets or regions do you primarily manage?
  * *Prompt 2:* How long have you been handling these overseas accounts, and how has international demand developed recently?
  * *Prompt 3:* How do you usually communicate with your foreign clients or distributors? Do you travel for meetings, or is it mostly online?
  * *Prompt 4:* Tell me about a challenging overseas deal, contract negotiation, or delivery issue you resolved recently. What happened?
  * *Evaluation Criteria:* Fluency, Present Simple vs Continuous consistency, Past Simple accuracy, Present Perfect with *since/for*, business vocabulary range.
* **00:20 – 00:30 (10 min): Spoken Diagnostic Part 2 — Negotiation & Strategic Scenarios**
  * *Prompt 1:* If you were responsible for opening up a completely new overseas market next year, which country would you target first and why?
  * *Prompt 2:* What are the biggest challenges when negotiating with international partners (cultural differences, pricing, lead times)?
  * *Prompt 3:* When you negotiate or write emails in English, where do you feel confident, and where do you feel you lack precision?
  * *Evaluation Criteria:* 2nd Conditional (*If I were... I would...*), modal verbs (*should, must, can*), diplomatic softeners, self-assessment awareness.

### Phase 2: Diagnostic Grammar Check (00:30 – 00:45)
* Screen-share the 15-question diagnostic test + 5 error-spotting items.
* Student reads and responds interactively.
* Teacher logs systemic errors vs slips without interrupting flow.

### Phase 3: Immediate High-Impact Review (00:45 – 00:55)
* Target Concept: **Present Perfect vs Past Simple in Commercial Communication**.
  * Rule: Specific past time (*yesterday, last month, in 2023*) $\rightarrow$ Past Simple.
  * Rule: Unfinished time / status up to now (*so far, already, yet, since, this week*) $\rightarrow$ Present Perfect.
  * Rapid oral drilling with sales sentences.

### Phase 4: Course Alignment & Next Steps (00:55 – 01:00)
* Highlight 2 key communication strengths observed.
* Propose 2 priority structural targets for upcoming classes.
* Agree on the 50/50 balance: Practical commercial speaking integrated with immediate grammar accuracy.

# Section 2: The Diagnostic Grammar Test (Worksheet & Answer Key)

### Multiple Choice Questions (15 Items)
1. **Routine vs Current Project:**  
   *Question:* Right now our sales department _______ a key contract with an overseas distributor, but normally we _______ regional retail accounts.  
   *Answer:* B) is negotiating / handle. *(Tests Present Continuous for temporary actions vs Present Simple for regular routines).*
2. **Narrative Interruption:**  
   *Question:* While the export team _______ the logistics documents, the foreign buyer suddenly _______ to amend the payment terms.  
   *Answer:* C) were preparing / asked. *(Tests Past Continuous background action interrupted by Past Simple).*
3. **Unfinished vs Finished Time:**  
   *Question:* I _______ with overseas distributors for five years now, but before joining this department I _______ domestic clients.  
   *Answer:* B) have worked / managed. *(Tests Present Perfect for ongoing state vs Past Simple for completed past period).*
4. **Experience vs Specific Past Event:**  
   *Question:* "_______ to a major international trade fair in Germany or the Middle East?" / "Yes, I _______ our company stand at an expo in Frankfurt two months ago."  
   *Answer:* A) Have you ever been / attended. *(Tests Present Perfect for life experience transitioned to Past Simple for specific time).*
5. **Duration of Ongoing Action:**  
   *Question:* Our export manager looks exhausted because she _______ with our partners in Singapore nonstop since 6:00 AM.  
   *Answer:* C) has been negotiating. *(Tests Present Perfect Continuous for duration leading up to present).*
6. **Instant Commercial Decision:**  
   *Question:* "Our client just sent an email refusing our shipping terms." / "Don't worry, I _______ their representative right away to clarify the situation."  
   *Answer:* A) will call. *(Tests "will" for spontaneous, on-the-spot decisions).*
7. **Strict Trade Regulations:**  
   *Question:* Under international export law, our department _______ provide an official certificate of origin before the shipment can clear customs.  
   *Answer:* B) must / has to. *(Tests strong objective obligation vs soft recommendation).*
8. **First Conditional in Sales:**  
   *Question:* If the overseas distributor _______ the full container load today, we _______ a 7% volume discount.  
   *Answer:* B) orders / will provide. *(Tests 1st conditional: If + present simple, will + base).*
9. **Second Conditional in Bargaining:**  
   *Question:* If our company _______ more flexible credit terms to foreign buyers, we _______ much faster market share abroad.  
   *Answer:* B) offered / would gain. *(Tests 2nd conditional: If + past simple, would + base for hypothetical commercial scenarios).*
10. **Third Conditional (Past Review):**  
    *Question:* If we _______ our sales pitch to their local business culture, we _______ the distribution tender last year.  
    *Answer:* B) had adapted / wouldn't have lost. *(Tests 3rd conditional: If + past perfect, would have + past participle for past unreal outcomes).*
11. **Passive Voice in Contracts:**  
    *Question:* All export shipments _______ strictly according to Incoterms before they _______ to the port of entry.  
    *Answer:* B) are insured / are dispatched. *(Tests present passive in formal logistics agreements).*
12. **Relative Clauses:**  
    *Question:* This is the foreign distributor _______ warehouse network covers the entire Latin American market.  
    *Answer:* C) whose. *(Tests possessive relative pronoun for corporate entities).*
13. **Verb Patterns:**  
    *Question:* The client considered _______ the contract for another two years, but they refused _______ the revised price index.  
    *Answer:* B) renewing / to accept. *(Tests consider + -ing vs refuse + to infinitive).*
14. **Diplomatic Indirect Questions:**  
    *Question:* "Could you clarify _______?"  
    *Answer:* B) when the overseas shipment will arrive. *(Tests non-inverted word order in indirect inquiries).*
15. **Dependent Prepositions:**  
    *Question:* Our team is responsible _______ developing overseas partnerships, and our quarterly targets depend heavily _______ market stability.  
    *Answer:* C) for / on. *(Tests responsible for, depend on).*

### Error-Spotting Challenge (5 Items)
1. *"I am working in the overseas division since January 2021."* $\rightarrow$ **have worked / have been working** (Duration with *since* requires Present Perfect).
2. *"We need to discuss about the pricing policy before the meeting..."* $\rightarrow$ **discuss the pricing policy** (No preposition *about* with transitive verb *discuss*).
3. *"If the distributor will sign the contract by Friday, we will dispatch..."* $\rightarrow$ **If the distributor signs** (No *will* in conditional time clauses).
4. *"I look forward to meet you at the trade exhibition next month."* $\rightarrow$ **look forward to meeting** (Preposition *to* followed by gerund).
5. *"The sales director explained me the new commission structure."* $\rightarrow$ **explained to me** (*explain something to someone*).

# Section 3: 50% Extra Extension Bank (~30 Minutes of Buffer)

### Situational Sales Dilemma A: The Price Cut Pressure
* **Scenario:** An established overseas distributor demands an immediate 15% discount across all vehicle/equipment lines due to currency devaluation in their domestic market, threatening to switch to a low-cost Asian manufacturer.
* **Roleplay / Debate:** Formulating a counter-proposal using conditional concessions (*"If we adjust payment milestones to 60 days, would you commit to maintaining order volume?"*).

### Situational Sales Dilemma B: Market Expansion Strategy
* **Scenario:** Entering a new export market: Exclusive regional distributor agreement vs. setting up a direct branch office. Evaluating risk, margin, and brand control.

### Commercial Conditionals Rapid-Fire Drill
1. If the overseas client signs the purchase order before the end of the month, we will...
2. If freight rates continue to rise over the next quarter, our export margins will...
3. If our management agreed to a 60-day payment term, foreign buyers would...
4. If you had the chance to visit any overseas market next month, which country would you...
5. If a shipment arrived damaged at a foreign port, what would be your first step?

### Commercial Passive Voice Transformations
1. *Active:* "Our export logistics team dispatches every shipment within 48 hours of payment."  
   $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* "Every shipment is dispatched within 48 hours of payment."
2. *Active:* "Customs officers held the container at the border due to missing documentation."  
   $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* "The container was held at the border due to missing documentation."
3. *Active:* "The finance department is currently reviewing the foreign partner's credit limit."  
   $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* "The foreign partner's credit limit is currently being reviewed."
4. *Active:* "We have already credited the disputed amount to your overseas account."  
   $\rightarrow$ *Passive:* "The disputed amount has already been credited to your overseas account."

### Homework / Bridge Task
* Draft an email to a foreign client requesting a 10% price cut: Decline politely, propose an alternative volume-based discount, and state terms clearly using 1st/2nd conditional structures.
